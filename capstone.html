Capstone Step 5 Video

Below is a video I recorded for the 5th and final step in my capstone process. This video now also contains my short walkthrough and comparison due to file size limitations. The goal of this video was quickly walk through my website and to answer these five questions;

  1. What do I call my interdisciplinary specialization?
  2. What was the topic of my capstone project and why did I choose it?
  3. What did each of my disciplines contribute to my intervention / project?
  4. How did i integrate disparate elements of my focus areas to create something uniquely interdisciplinary?
  5. Explain what my work on this project can tell viewers about myself as a worker, researcher, and communicator.

Relevant Research

Much research went into the development of this website and understanding important aspects of UX / UI design and writing as they apply to web development. In this section, I will share some relevant quotes and information which directly impacted this capstone project.

Dr. Anita Gaul, a professor of Southwest Minnesota State University, comments on the importance of writing in history “in history, like in most other fields of study, writing is the primary form of communication”. Gaul also quotes “employers value employees who can write well, summarize information, evaluate evidence, and make reasoned arguments” (Gaul). This is where my justification for the intersection of my disciplines comes from, writing is an integral part of history and played a role in the development of a more concise website.

Carrie Cousins, a freelance writer and designer with 15 years of professional experience, writes on the importance of UX writing in the web design process, “without good UX writing, your design projects will be flat, lack voice and personality, and will provide a subpar experience for users” (Cousins). Cousins then briefly explains what UX writing is “good UX writing guides visitors through a website interface in an intuitive way”(Cousins). This was another area of focus when developing my project; I did not want to eliminate the personality of the existing BRAG website, instead I wanted to give users an easier time navigating the website. Especially when it came to the adoption process.

Felipe Guimarães, design leader and founder of Aela.io, makes a similar finding about UX writing. He writes "the primary goal of UX writing is to build efficient communication between a design and the people who will interact with it” (Guimarães). This was another guiding piece of understanding UX / UI writing, which was a completely foriegn topic to me. I knew that I must change the written content of the website, along with its design, so that users could have an easier time finding the information they want / need.

Oftentimes, as web designers tend to focus on aesthetics and functions, the task of writing the literal content of a site is left to inexperienced clients. These are the findings of Matt Saunders, a web designer and writer with over 15 years of experience. He explains “when embarking on a new website project, designers tend to focus on the aesthetics and functionality of their work. This means that content writing is a task often pushed onto the client to fulfil. The unfortunate consequence of this decision is that the website’s content ultimately comes in too late, in the wrong format, and of poor quality” (Saunders). This was an extremely relevant finding to my capstone topic. The original BRAG website is clearly ran by someone who lacks experience with professional writing and web design. This means that some of their content is not as good and professional as it sould be, damaging the overall image of the organization.

When thinking about the combination of historical study and computer science related studies, it is apparent that there really is not much overlap between the interests of these two fields. However, Steven Bradley, founder of vanseo design and freelance web designer, finds an interesting intersection between the two fields in the growing field of design history. Bradley offers several reasons for studying design history; he claims that studying the history of design will help you to become a better designer, allow you to connect past, present, and future, and will help you to acquire better taste overall (Bradley). This was an interesting find, and relevant to one of my favorite aspects of my website. The "hero image" I mention in my videos (the image that displays on every page when you open it) was a design choice that I saw on another website similar to the BRAG one. I liked it so much that I decided my website had to have one, it seemed to be the perfect way to grab the attention of any dog lover.

Vitaly Friedman, editor-in-chief of Smashing Magazine and UX professional, writes in an article about the importance of user centric design and its overall impact on a website “usability and the utility, not the visual design, determine the success or failure of a website” (Friedman). I only partly agree with this claim. Of course, a website that does not serve its primary function is probably doomed for failure. However, I feel that Friedman undervalues the stylist aspects of websites a little. For example, BRAG's website functions well enough, however I feel that the look and style of the websites design give it a negative connotation which harms the overall user experience just as much as poor functionality would. Regardless, I still aimed to streamline the "function" of finding an animal and easily locating the information required to adopt while designing my website.

In his excerpt from the Smashing book, Vitaly Friedman comments on the ideas that modern day websites have lost a sense of personality and how personality can impact human experiences with one's brand / website. He explains “generic web layouts have become somewhat of a misnomer in conversations circling around web design these days. We’re bored and slightly annoyed by how predictable and uninspired most web experiences have become” (Friedman). This was another area of information which drove my web design process. I wanted to keep the personality of the original BRAG website, which it has plenty of, while still streamlining the content of the site. One example of this in my work would be the hero image i've mentioned. It is a picture of my dog alphie, who came from BRAG! This choice, when presenting my work to my capstone class, had the exact effect that I wanted it to have. Everyone enjoyed seeing the picture and suggested that I play on this sense of personality, something I plan to add on to this website in the future.

Chris Bradley, co-founder of DoubleDome digital marketing, writes on the impact of professional web design and visual appeal “Within seconds of landing on a website, users form their first impression. This impression is heavily influenced by the visual appeal of the cite” (Bradley). Findings like this one were a heavy influence on my design choices. I feel that the current BRAG website neglects this idea, its initial impression is overwhelmingly unprofessional and underwhelming. Thus, I tried my hardest to change this effect with my design choices, especially with my hero image. I feel that I represented area of research the most within my design choices.

Kara Jensen, creative principal at BOP Design, makes similar findings about the “look and feel” of websites. She breaks down looks and feels into two separate categories, with look being visual components of a website like colors, images, fonts, and layout. Feel is described as first impressions, movement, and interactivity. Jensen explains the importance of the looks and feels of your website to users “your B2B website’s overall look and feel is important because it instantly conveys an attitude to your clients before they even start reading any content” (Jensen). Again, another industry professional finds that the aesthetics of a website are incredibly important to users experiences, especially when considering a first impression. I took information like this very seriously, as aesthetic design is an area which I struggle in. Thus, I tried my hardest to keep things neat, stylish, and without too much of a sacrafice in personality.

Overall, all of the above points played some sort of role in influencing my personal design choices. From my research, it was clear that my website must contain professionally written content, personality, good first looks, functionality, and much more. It was really interesting to see how all of these findings could actively play a role in the development of my website and how they could serve to effect my capstone topic. This being said, I am nowhere near being an industry professional or an active voice in the conversation. I still have much to learn about all of these topics and more.
